Links: the map and the reputation

Links play two roles. Inside your site, they form a map. From other sites, they build a reputation.

By Nada Iddouch · 9 November 2026 · 2 min read

Key points

  • Internal links help Google and your visitors find their way.
  • One link from a serious site is worth more than a hundred doubtful ones.
  • Buying links to manipulate Google is against its rules.

Internal links: the city map

An internal link goes from one page of your site to another. Without links, a page is like a house with no road. Nobody gets there.

In our guitar shop, the article “How to tune a guitar” can lead to the tuners page. The reader gets help. Google understands how the two connect.

Links that mean something

The clickable text is called the anchor. “Click here” says nothing. “Choosing your first guitar” says everything, to the reader and to Google.

Keep your important pages close

An important page should be reachable in a few clicks from the home page. If it is hidden deep in the site, Google and your customers will see it less.

External links: the reputation

When another site links to yours, it is a kind of recommendation. Like in real life, it all depends on who is recommending. A well-known music magazine is worth far more than an unknown site that never talks about guitars.

How to get them

The best method is simple: create something worth citing. A really useful guide. A study. A free tool.

Avoid buying links to climb in Google. It is against its rules, and it can cost you dearly.

Test yourself

You have just published a very good article, but no page on your site leads to it. What do you do first?

You add internal links from pages about the same topic.
