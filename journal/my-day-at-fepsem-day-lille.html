My day at FePSeM Day in Lille

Today I’m at FePSeM Day in Lille, a day dedicated to search marketing. Here’s why I’m going, the questions I’m bringing with me and what I took away from the day.

Today, off to Lille

This Friday, 20 November, I’m in Lille for FePSeM Day, at Espace Inkermann Châtillon. A full day dedicated to search marketing: talks, case studies and plenty of conversations between practitioners.

FePSeM is the Fédération des Professionnels du Search Marketing, the French federation of search marketing professionals. It brings together people who, like me, spend their days asking how to be found, understood and chosen online. This edition’s programme covers exactly what I’m working on right now: SEO, SEA, GEO, artificial intelligence and e-commerce.

The programme

From 9 am to 5 pm, the day runs through talks and a round table around one common thread: generative AI applied to search marketing and e-commerce. Here are the announced sessions:

  • Opening keynote
  • “Is GEO only the SEO team’s job?” — Smartkeyword
  • “How can you control the content AI systems use?” — Neper
  • “AI: the new battleground for e-merchants” — NOIISE
  • “Built-in AI: how far can you go with AI and MCP?” (2:45 pm)
  • Round table “AI and e-commerce: what major retailers are preparing for 2027” — with Leroy Merlin and La Redoute

Why I’m going

SEO and e-commerce are my day-to-day work. But in a field that moves this fast, expertise doesn’t maintain itself: without comparing your practice with others’, you end up going round in circles inside your own certainties.

A day like this is the opposite. You talk with professionals working on other sites, other industries, other constraints. You compare. You find out what others are testing before it’s written everywhere.

And the timing matters. Search is changing fast: AI-generated answers, conversational engines, where brands fit in those new answers… Our profession is changing deeply. I’d rather anticipate that change by talking with the people living it than just put up with it.

The questions I’m bringing

I’m arriving with a few questions in mind, the ones that come up most often in my work:

  • GEO: how can a brand show up in AI answers, and how do you actually measure it?
  • E-commerce: what still works, and what is changing, when it comes to ranking product and category pages?
  • Data: which metrics should you track when some clicks disappear in favour of direct answers?
  • AI day to day: how do you use it to save time without losing quality or a voice of your own?

How I approach a conference

Over time I’ve picked up a few habits. I don’t try to write everything down: I only keep the ideas I can test in the weeks that follow. A conference is only worth it to me if it turns into action.

I also pay as much attention to the breaks as to the talks. That’s often where the most useful exchanges happen: a remark by the coffee machine, a story shared without slides, a new person to follow.

What I took away

As usual, I didn’t write everything down: I kept what I can actually apply. Here is, session by session, what struck me most.

AI bots: you don’t block “ChatGPT”

This session went back to where everything starts: crawling. There’s a lot of talk about GEO, but before an AI can cite your content, it first has to be able to read it. If a bot can’t access a page, that page doesn’t exist for it.

One line stuck with me: “You don’t block ChatGPT, you block one of OpenAI’s bots.” Each company runs several bots, and each one has a specific job:

  • Training (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Meta-ExternalAgent): collects the web to train future models. Blocked, your content leaves the next datasets.
  • AI search (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot): indexes the web so the assistant can cite sources. Blocked, you disappear from answers and citations.
  • Search engine + AI (Googlebot, Bingbot, Applebot): a classic search index, reused by its AI. Blocked, you also lose organic search.
  • On demand (ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User): fetches a page when a user asks. Blocked, the assistant can no longer read the page.
  • Agent (Google-Agent, Meta-ExternalFetcher): browses and acts on sites on the user’s behalf. Blocked, delegated tasks fail on your site.
  • Control token (Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended): crawls nothing, but governs how already-collected data is used. Blocked, you opt out of training, with no effect on search.

The per-company detail was very telling. At OpenAI, ChatGPT-User may ignore robots.txt, and a fourth bot, OAI-AdsBot, only visits ad landing pages. At Anthropic, all three bots respect robots.txt, even on demand, and the old names Claude-Web and anthropic-ai are obsolete: blocking them no longer does anything.

Bing separates training and grounding

Another important piece of news: at Bing, NoArchive and NoCache are becoming obsolete as AI preferences. Bing now distinguishes two things: training (set in robots.txt or page by page, allowed by default) and grounding, meaning using a page to generate an answer (set page by page only, through a meta tag or the X-Robots-Tag header).

The announced timeline: temporary preferences in Bing Webmaster Tools from 15 December 2026, new preferences read as pages are crawled from 1 February 2027, then, on 1 March 2027, NoArchive and NoCache will no longer count as AI preferences: grounding will be allowed by default.

The blocking you don’t see: CDN, Cloudflare and WAF

This is the point that struck me most. You can have a carefully thought-out robots.txt and still block AI bots without knowing it.

A concrete example with Cloudflare: when you connect a domain, it suggests recommended AI settings (allow search, block agents on pages with ads, disallow training) and an option, Bot Preference Sync, that adds its own directives at the top of your robots.txt. In other words, Cloudflare can change your robots.txt on the fly:

# BEGIN Cloudflare Bot Preference Sync
User-agent: GPTBot
User-agent: ClaudeBot
User-agent: meta-externalagent
User-agent: Amazonbot
User-agent: Google-Extended
User-agent: Applebot-Extended
Disallow: /
# END Cloudflare Bot Preference Sync

User-agent: *
Disallow: /wp-admin/

And that’s not the only possible block: the CDN can filter some bots before they ever reach the site, and the WAF (web application firewall) can mistake a legitimate bot for a threat. The result: SEO thinks the door is open, while the infrastructure has shut it. It’s something you fix with the technical teams, not on your own.

AI: the new battleground for e-merchants

A very hands-on session that put two ideas side by side: GEO (getting a brand into AI answers, with reasons the customer understands) and GEA (buying a sponsored presence in those answers). The figures, from October 2026 tracking, confirm and sharpen what I see in the field: in ChatGPT’s product carousels, 84.4% of purchase links lead to a product page, and an AI can recommend a brand without ever citing its website.

There was so much in it that I wrote a separate article: GEO, explained: what changes for SEO and what you can do right now.

Three baskets for AI at work

A simple framework for deciding what to hand over to AI, which I find useful well beyond search:

  • Red basket, AI alone: logging leads in the CRM, writing meeting notes, planning next steps, spotting what’s stalling, keeping tasks and reporting up to date.
  • Orange basket, AI prepares and a human validates: drafting follow-ups, preparing a meeting, pricing a proposal, forecasting the quarter.
  • Green basket, 100% human: the meeting itself, the negotiation, the decision to send, the relationship.

And a line I’m keeping: “What a human always forgets shouldn’t depend on a human.”

What I’m going to do

Back home, I want to check on the sites I work on: which AI bots the robots.txt really allows, type by type; the Cloudflare, CDN and firewall settings, especially any automatic robots.txt sync; that price, stock, specs and reviews actually appear in the HTML served, without JavaScript; and the server logs, to see which bots really come through. Exactly the kind of ideas I like to bring back from a conference: concrete and testable straight away.
