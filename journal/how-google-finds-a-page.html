How Google finds and ranks a page

Before talking about keywords, you need to understand one simple thing: how Google works.

By Nada Iddouch · 9 November 2026 · 2 min read

Key points

  • Google does three things: it explores, it files, it ranks.
  • If your page is not in the index, it cannot appear.
  • AI answers rely on the same basics.

Imagine a huge library

Google is like a giant library. Billions of pages are stored in it. When you ask a question, the librarian picks the best pages for you.

Let’s take an example. You open a small online guitar shop. You want Google to show your site when someone searches for “guitar for beginners”. For that, three steps have to happen.

Step 1: Google explores

Google sends robots across the web. They go from link to link, just like you do when you click from one page to another. This is called crawling.

If no link leads to your page, the robot has little chance of finding it.

Step 2: Google files

The robot reads the page. Google tries to understand its topic, then stores it in its index. The index is the library’s catalogue.

Be careful: not every page gets stored. Google does not guarantee it. An empty, copied or useless page can be left out.

Step 3: Google ranks

Someone types “guitar for beginners”. Google looks in its index and picks the most useful pages. It looks at many things: the meaning of the search, the quality of the page, the person’s country, language and device.

What about AI answers?

Google sometimes shows a summary written by AI at the top of the results, with links to pages. Google says there is nothing special to do to appear there. The page just has to be indexed and able to show up in normal results. So the basics stay the same.

Test yourself

Your page does not appear on Google at all. What is the first thing to check?

Whether it is in the index. A page that has not been filed cannot be shown, even if it is excellent.
