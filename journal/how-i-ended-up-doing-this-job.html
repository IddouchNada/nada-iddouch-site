How I ended up doing this job

In this first article, I look back at the path that took me from accounting to digital, and then to a job built between analysis, creativity, SEO, e-commerce and experimentation.

By Nada Iddouch · 8 July 2026 · 6 min read

Key points

  • I started out wanting to become a chartered accountant, before realising I needed more creativity.
  • My taste for numbers never left me; it turned into a strong interest in data and analysis.
  • Digital marketing, SEO and e-commerce opened up a space where analysis and creativity could meet.
  • My first attempts, even imperfect ones, helped me confirm the direction I wanted to follow.

At the start

I am very happy to write this first article, because it marks the beginning of a space where I can share my path, my thoughts and what I learn over time.

To begin, I wanted to answer a simple question: how did I end up doing this job today?

After high school, I wanted to become a chartered accountant. Yes, dream big or go home: with me, it has often been like that. I liked the idea of a solid, structured and demanding profession, and I already had a real taste for numbers.

The turning point

After my first real work experience, I understood that I could not spend my whole life in accounting. I needed creativity: to invent, to test, to get things wrong and to start again.

Accounting still left me with something very precious: a real relationship with numbers. Today, it still helps me enormously. Understanding data, cross-checking it, bringing out results or hidden insights: that has become something I deeply love.

Discovering digital

So I decided to change my master’s and move towards digital marketing. That choice did not come by chance. I came across Neil Patel’s videos, and for a while I watched them almost every evening. I began to understand how the web, SEO, content, ranking and internal linking worked.

I created a site in HTML to teach myself, and I started exploring this world with a lot of curiosity. To tell the truth, during Covid, between e-commerce, the web and the guitar, I barely saw it go by, even in a small space. I was already busy discovering another world.

First attempts

At the same time, I had started a master’s in digital marketing and international trade. E-commerce fascinated me immediately. You discover strategies, models, stories of brands that start from almost nothing and build something big. I found that extremely inspiring.

I could also see myself starting at my own scale, from my studio flat, with an idea to test. I loved pouring, and I said to myself: why not create phone cases inspired by abstract art? Yes, in my head, I was a bit of a Picasso.

But behind this idea, what I really liked was understanding how to sell online with Shopify, how an e-commerce model works, how to launch a product and how to learn by building. I was also starting to understand better everything Neil Patel explained about SEO and visibility.

What that period taught me

My project did not take off. Looking back, that is not very surprising: e-commerce takes time, money, patience and consistency. At that moment, I did not have all of that yet.

But even if this first try did not work the way I imagined, it confirmed one essential thing: this really was the world I wanted to explore.

What came next

I then did my internship at HD Soudage, and the adventure has continued ever since. That is where many things began to take on a more concrete meaning. And alongside work, I keep testing a huge number of things.

Over time, I understood that sometimes you have to let your intuition guide you and step off the most standard paths. You have to test, dare, experiment, and accept that not everything will be perfectly linear from the start.

What I keep today

A poem by Robert Frost has always inspired me: The Road Not Taken. The idea that a path is also built through forks, detours and less obvious choices speaks to me deeply.

If I had to sum up the start of this journey, I would simply say this: I ended up in this job while looking for a space where analysis, creativity, data, digital and the desire to build could finally come together.
