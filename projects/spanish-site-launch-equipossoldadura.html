Case study: building a Spanish e-commerce site from scratch in two months

equipossoldadura.es: an industrial e-commerce site for the Spanish market, designed and developed entirely in-house in two months, alongside the French site and while training an apprentice.

By Nada Iddouch · 25 November 2026 · 5 min read

2 monthsfrom design to launch
100%developed in-house, to stay in control and keep costs down
2 sitesrun in parallel, French and Spanish

In short

  • An e-commerce site for Spain built from scratch on PrestaShop, the same technology as the French site.
  • Everything was developed in-house: more control, and significant savings.
  • UX, tech, shipping, industry knowledge and Google Ads: a cross-functional project delivered in two months, while training an apprentice.
  • The site has just gone live; the work now focuses on content.

The context

To open up the Spanish market, the company needed a dedicated e-commerce site, designed for welding professionals in Spain: equipossoldadura.es.

We chose PrestaShop, the same technology as the French site. That choice let us build on everything created during the French site’s redesign: platform know-how, catalogue organisation and SEO best practices.

Choosing to do it in-house

Rather than handing the project to an agency, we developed everything in-house. Two reasons: keeping control of the site, so it can evolve fast without outside dependency, and keeping the budget in check. Bringing all the technical work in-house delivered significant savings.

What I did: far more than the technical side

I built this site from scratch, with the help of an apprentice. Launching an e-commerce site isn’t just about putting pages online: every function had to be covered at once.

  • Site creation and architecture: catalogue structure, categories, key pages.
  • UX design: journeys designed for professional buyers looking for a precise reference who want to order easily.
  • Technical: configuration and development on PrestaShop, entirely in-house.
  • Shipping: finding the right delivery solution for Spain and setting it up. Without reliable shipping, there is no e-commerce site.
  • Industry knowledge: adapting the offer to a technical industrial sector, where buyers expect precision and trust.
  • Google Ads: structuring and launching the first campaigns to support the launch.

The real challenge: running two sites at once

The most demanding part wasn’t any single task, it was doing everything at the same time. Over those two months, I built the Spanish site while still making sure my day-to-day work on the French site ran smoothly: performance tracking, SEO, campaigns and everyday requests.

That takes rigorous organisation and clear priorities: knowing what can’t wait on the existing site, and what moves the new one forward.

Welcoming and training an apprentice

Alongside the project, I welcomed and trained an apprentice who worked with me on it. Onboarding them, passing on the basics of e-commerce and PrestaShop, then gradually handing over tasks: that is also what made it possible to move so fast. Training someone during a launch is demanding, but it’s an investment that benefits the whole team.

Where the site stands

The site has just gone live. The technical side is in place; we’re now working on content: product pages, categories and Spanish-language content. The goal is to build visibility over time, with the same method as on the French site: healthy foundations rather than one-off pushes.

What I take away

  • Keeping the technical work in-house means keeping control, speed and a grip on costs.
  • An e-commerce launch is a cross-functional project: UX, tech, logistics, industry knowledge and acquisition have to move together.
  • You can move fast without sacrificing what already exists, as long as priorities are clear.
  • Passing on knowledge is part of the project: training someone is also building what comes next.
